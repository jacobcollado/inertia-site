## Why imagery comes first

Product shots tell people what you sell. Imagery tells them why they want it.

Aether is designed around large, full-bleed images: the hero, the collection banners, the editorial sections. Put strong imagery in those spaces and the whole store comes together. Put weak or generic imagery in them and even a well-built store feels empty.

Strong imagery does one job: it makes someone stop and look. Everything below is about how to get there.

## What strong imagery looks like

These references share a few things worth copying.

### It has a mood, not just a subject

Look at the difference between "a person wearing a jacket" and "a person in a jacket at night, lit by a flash, leaning on a red car." The second one has a feeling. Shoppers remember feelings, not garments.

### It looks lived in

Flash at night. Film grain. A slightly awkward pose on a worn armchair. The most compelling imagery often feels like a moment someone caught, not a set someone built. Imperfection reads as real, and real reads as trustworthy.

### It keeps the backdrop simple

A grey curtain. A plain wall. A dark street. When the background is quiet, the clothing and the person carry the image. Two of the references use the exact same curtain, and together they feel like one campaign.

### It's consistent

Pick a look and commit to it: the same film stock or edit, the same kind of light, the same backdrop or setting. A consistent set of average images beats a random set of great ones. Consistency is what makes a store feel like a brand.

### It shows the product in context

Every reference shows the product on a person, in a place. That answers the question a flat product shot can't: what it looks like on someone, and who that someone is.

## Where it goes in Aether

- **Hero:** your single strongest image. Use the full-width layout and let the image fill the screen. If the image is dark, turn on the transparent header.
- **Collection banners:** one image per collection that sets its mood.
- **Mega menu:** a featured image on the right side of the menu gives people a reason to open it.
- **Between product grids:** editorial sections break up long collections and remind people what the brand is about.

Keep flat, white-background product shots for product pages and grid cards. Keep mood imagery for everywhere else. Mixing the two is what makes a store feel both premium and easy to shop.

## Getting imagery made

- **Shoot a small campaign** with each drop. One location, one look, a few outfits. Twenty strong images will fill your store for months.
- **Film or a film-style edit** is an easy way to get the lived-in feel on a budget.
- **Brief the photographer with references.** Share the exact images you're drawn to. It saves a reshoot.
- **Need help with the creative?** See *Designers we trust*.

Only use imagery you own or have permission to use. Images from other brands, even as placeholders, can get a store taken down.
