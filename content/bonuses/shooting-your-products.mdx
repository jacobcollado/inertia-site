## The shot you're aiming for

For product pages and collection grids, the most reliable shot is simple: **the product laid flat, shot from directly above, on a solid white background.**

It works because it removes every distraction. Nothing competes with the product, every item in a collection lines up with the next, and Aether's layouts, built around clean product cards, look the way they were designed to.

Shoot every product the same way. Consistency across a collection is what makes a store look professional, more than any single great photo.

## What you need

You don't need a studio. You need three things.

### 1. Lighting

Lighting matters more than the camera. It's the difference between a product that looks premium and one that looks like a listing.

- **Get a pair of standing LED lights.** Professional-level kits with softboxes or diffusers are on Amazon for a fraction of what studio gear costs, and they do the same job.
- **Light from both sides**, slightly above the product, angled down. This cancels out shadows.
- **Turn off overhead room lights** and keep daylight off the product. Mixed light sources give you uneven, off-color shots.
- **Diffuse everything.** Hard, direct light shows every crease and makes harsh shadows. If your kit didn't come with diffusers, a thin white sheet in front of the lights works.

### 2. A white background

A white background gives your product maximum contrast and makes editing easy.

- **Foam board or a white backdrop** is best: flat and consistent.
- **A white sheet or blanket** works too. Iron it or pull it tight, because wrinkles show up in every shot.
- Make it bigger than you think you need, so the edges of the frame stay white.

### 3. Something to shoot with

A recent phone is enough.

- **Shoot from directly above**, with the phone parallel to the floor. A tilted angle distorts the product's shape.
- **Stand on something stable** or use an overhead phone mount so every shot is framed the same.
- **Don't use digital zoom.** Move closer instead.
- **Tap to focus** on the product and lock exposure so the white doesn't blow out.

## Laying out the product

- **Style it like it's being worn or used.** Arrange clothing so it has shape: sleeves slightly angled, hems straight.
- **Steam or lint-roll first.** Every fiber shows at full size on a product page.
- **Leave space around the edges** so you can crop consistently.
- **Shoot the details too:** labels, hardware, stitching, texture. These are the shots that answer a shopper's questions before they have to ask.

## Before you upload

- **Crop every image to the same ratio** so your collection grid lines up.
- **Keep file names descriptive**, like `black-leather-jacket-front.jpg`. It helps search.
- **Upload the same angle first** for every product: front, then back, then details.

## If you can't shoot yet: AI as a last resort

If you don't have the equipment yet, AI image tools can turn a rough photo into a clean, white-background product shot. Where they are today, the results can look genuinely good.

We don't recommend this long term, for one important reason: **the product won't be one to one.** Graphics can shift, fabric texture can change, colors can drift, small details can be invented or lost. A customer who receives something that doesn't match the photo is a customer who asks for a refund.

If you use AI:

- Treat it as **temporary**, until you can shoot the real thing.
- **Compare every image to the actual product** before it goes live, especially logos, prints and hardware.
- **Keep at least one real photo** on each product page so there's an honest reference.

Then replace them with real shots as soon as you can.
